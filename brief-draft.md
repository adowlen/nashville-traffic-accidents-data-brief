# Nashville traffic accidents: pedestrians are 30.2 percent of the people killed in reported crashes, and a reported crash in dark, unlit conditions is about five times as likely to be a fatal crash as one in daylight

**One-line finding:** Of the 926 people killed in Nashville reported crashes from January 1, 2019 to October 31, 2026, 280 (30.2 percent) were pedestrians, and the fatal-crash share of reported crashes coded DARK - NOT LIGHTED is 1.14 percent (143 of 12,565) against 0.22 percent in DAYLIGHT (297 of 134,199).

Source: Metro Nashville open data, Traffic Accidents layer (Traffic_Accidents_2, 222,903 records pulled November 2, 2026), maintained from the Metro Nashville Police Department (MNPD) TITAN system. One row is one traffic accident reported to MNPD. The layer's own metadata (Traffic Accidents Metadata v2.0, May 6, 2024) states that the export is not an official MNPD accident report and is subject to change during investigation.

Four definitions govern everything below. First, every figure is a count or share of REPORTED crashes. The layer carries no traffic-volume, vehicle-miles, or trip denominator, so this brief computes no per-mile or per-trip rate and makes no risk claim of that kind; where a share is printed, its population is printed with it. Second, severity is printed on two named bases: the all-reported basis (222,902 rows with injury and fatality counts populated; one empty stub row is excluded from severity denominators and counted in the row total) and the fully-coded basis (200,695 rows with collision, weather, and illumination all coded). Third, the window is January 1, 2019 to October 31, 2026 (America/Chicago) as served; the metadata describes the source system as running from 2010, but the served layer starts in 2019, and 2026 is a partial year that is only ever compared on the January-October frame. Fourth, involvement of a pedestrian or a pedalcycle is read from the layer's harmful-event codes (HarmfulCodes), not from its description strings, for the reason recorded in Finding 2.

## Finding 1: Reported crashes fell after 2019 and stayed in a lower band, while fatal crashes rose to 125-126 in 2022-2023 before returning to 100-103

The layer holds 222,903 reported crashes pulled in full on November 2, 2026 (server count and local count equal). Across the window, 67,953 crashes recorded at least one injury (30.5 percent of the 222,902 rows with counts populated; 32.5 percent on the fully-coded basis) and 865 crashes recorded at least one fatality. The people totals are 95,219 people injured and 926 people killed; people totals are never divided into crash shares in this brief.

By year, reported crashes were 37,918 in 2019, then 27,408 in 2020, 28,003 in 2021, 26,319 in 2022, 27,102 in 2023, 26,155 in 2024, and 26,985 in 2025. Fatal crashes moved the other way across the first part of that window: 100 in 2019, 108 in 2020, 118 in 2021, 125 in 2022, 126 in 2023, then 103 in 2024 and 100 in 2025. Injury crashes held a 7,462 to 9,189 band in 2020-2025 after 9,682 in 2019. On the January-October frame, the only frame on which the partial year is compared, 2026 recorded 23,012 crashes and 85 fatal crashes against 22,359 crashes and 81 fatal crashes in January-October 2025.

The claim in this finding is the two measured series: reported volume lower after 2019, fatal-crash counts higher in 2020-2023 than in 2019 and back at the 2019 level in 2024-2025. Whether the mix of crashes that gets reported changed across the window is the strongest alternative explanation and is carried, unresolved, in the "What this might mean" section.

## Finding 2: Pedestrians are 1.5 percent of reported crashes and 30.2 percent of the people killed

Crashes with a pedestrian in the harmful-event codes number 3,330, which is 1.5 percent of reported crashes. Of those 3,330 crashes, 2,984 (89.6 percent) are injury crashes and 278 are fatal crashes, and the people killed in them number 280, which is 30.2 percent of all 926 people killed in reported crashes in the window. Pedalcycle-involved crashes (harmful-event code 9) number 530: 485 (91.5 percent) are injury crashes and 8 people were killed.

The codes are the measure because the layer's two harmful-event fields disagree in one place: the pedestrian code and the pedestrian description string agree exactly (3,330 crashes both ways), while the pedalcycle code appears on 530 rows and the string PEDALCYCLE appears on 0 rows of the description field. Involvement in this brief is therefore codes-only, and pedalcycle figures carry that label wherever they appear.

## Finding 3: Per reported crash, the fatal-crash share in dark, unlit conditions is about five times the daylight share

By the layer's illumination field, the fatal-crash share of reported crashes is 0.22 percent in DAYLIGHT (297 fatal crashes of 134,199 reported crashes), 0.38 percent at DUSK (15 of 3,946), 0.47 percent at DAWN (12 of 2,538), 0.76 percent in DARK - LIGHTED conditions (393 of 51,640), and 1.14 percent in DARK - NOT LIGHTED conditions (143 of 12,565). The dark-not-lighted share is about 5 times the daylight share, per reported crash. Injury-crash shares sit in a narrow band across the same conditions, from 32.2 percent in daylight to 34.2 percent in dark-not-lighted conditions, so the gradient is in the fatal share, not in whether a crash records an injury.

This is severity per reported crash, not risk per mile driven in the dark. The layer has no exposure measure by lighting condition (miles driven in each condition), and darkness as coded here travels with hour of day and road type, neither of which this layer separates from the lighting code. The ordering of the fatal-crash shares (daylight lowest, dark-not-lighted highest) also holds when only the complete years 2019-2025 are used, as recorded in the study this brief refreshes. The chart for this finding prints the buckets that are not charted (unknown (null) 13,806 crashes; UNKNOWN 2,531; Dark-Unknown Lighting 1,393; OTHER 284) so no bucket is hidden.

## Finding 4: HEAD-ON is the most severe coded collision type per crash, and the non-collision class carries the most fatal crashes

By collision type, HEAD-ON crashes (5,544) record the highest severity per reported crash among coded types: 56.1 percent are injury crashes and 1.57 percent are fatal crashes (87 fatal crashes, 106 people killed). The class NOT COLLISION W/MOTOR VEHICLE-TRANSPORT (37,857 crashes) is 36.8 percent injury and 1.34 percent fatal-crash, and it carries the most fatal crashes of any class, 509, with 525 people killed; it is the class in which most pedestrian and fixed-object deaths sit in this coding. ANGLE crashes (58,377) are 37.5 percent injury and 0.27 percent fatal-crash. The largest class, Front to Rear (65,984 crashes), is 34.1 percent injury and 0.11 percent fatal-crash (73 fatal crashes).

One bucket is excluded from every severity comparison in this finding, and the exclusion is the point: 13,126 crashes in the severity tables carry no collision type (13,127 rows in the full pull), and they record zero injuries and zero fatalities. Finding 8 records why: those rows are a minimally-coded subpopulation whose detail fields were never coded, so their zeros are definitional. They are printed in the package's data panels and named on the collision chart, and they are never presented as a safe category. A one-row category, REAR END (n = 1), is likewise not charted.

## Finding 5: Hit-and-run is 27.1 percent of reported crashes with the field populated, up from 22.1 percent in 2019

The hit-and-run field is populated on 222,891 of 222,903 rows (12 rows carry no value). Of those, 60,336 are recorded Y, which is 27.1 percent of reported crashes with the field populated (28.6 percent would be the share on the fully-coded basis; this brief uses the populated-field basis and names it). By year, the Y share was 22.1 percent in 2019 and has sat in a 27-30 percent band from 2021 on. Hit-and-run crashes are less often injury crashes than other reported crashes: 18.0 percent of hit-and-run crashes record an injury, against 35.1 percent of crashes recorded N. That pairing is printed as recorded; a minor-crash-heavy mix among hit-and-run reports is one reading of it, and the layer does not record crash circumstances that would separate readings.

## Finding 6: On known-precinct rows, Madison and Southeast record the highest injury-crash shares and Central the lowest

Precinct is recorded on 201,686 rows and not recorded on 21,217 (9.5 percent). On known rows only, the injury-crash share by precinct is: Madison 33.6 percent (19,186 crashes, 97 fatal crashes), Southeast 33.5 percent (27,179 crashes, 115 fatal crashes), Hermitage 31.4 percent (25,551; 103), North 30.9 percent (20,842; 104), South 29.8 percent (28,566; 127), West 29.8 percent (19,314; 67), East 28.8 percent (16,540; 63), Midtown Hills 28.4 percent (26,311; 56), and Central 24.5 percent (18,197; 43). Served precinct labels are truncated to six characters in the layer; the full MNPD precinct names are used here under the mapping recorded in stats.json.

These shares are cross-sectional, all-window, and known-rows only, for a stated reason: the not-recorded share of precinct falls across the window, from about 12-13 percent of rows in 2019-2023 to 8.6 percent in 2024, 2.4 percent in 2025, and 0.05 percent in 2026, so a precinct's share of crashes can rise simply because its rows stopped being unrecorded. No precinct trend is claimed, and no per-capita or per-mile precinct rate is computed, because the layer carries no precinct exposure denominator.

By ZIP code, the largest crash counts are 37013 (26,126 crashes, 104 fatal crashes) and 37211 (22,805 crashes, 105 fatal crashes). By the normalized intersection key (the two sides of the served address string sorted, because the raw strings count one junction under both name orders), the largest junction totals by injury crashes are Briley Pkwy N/S (788 crashes, 269 injury crashes) and Nolensville Pike / Nolensville Rd (744 crashes, 250 injury crashes). Intersection entries that name interstate ramp pseudo-addresses are system buckets spanning many physical ramps, not single ramps, and are labeled as served in the data panels; the junk bucket "UNKNOWN RAMP & ROAD" (1,265 rows) is excluded from the named lists and counted in stats.json.

## Finding 7: Excluding the midnight placeholder rows, reported crashes peak at 17:00, and the weekend fatal-crash share is higher than the weekday share

17,709 rows (7.9 percent) are stamped exactly 00:00 local time. That stamp is treated in this package as an unknown-time placeholder: its dates are used and its times are not, and every time-of-day figure excludes those rows (the audit records the polarity check: all flagged rows are exactly 00:00, and the 3,549 unflagged hour-0 rows carry real minutes). On the remaining rows, reported crashes peak in the 17:00 hour. The fatal-crash share of reported crashes is 0.55 percent on weekends against 0.34 percent on weekdays. Both figures are per reported crash on the stated populations; no trip or volume denominator by hour or by day type exists in this layer.

## Finding 8: Three field-level limits bound every figure above, and each is printed with its count

First, the minimally-coded subpopulation: 13,127 rows are null on collision type, weather, AND illumination (the collision-null set and the all-three-null set are the same rows), their harmful-event codes are null on 99.9 percent of them, their hit-and-run Y rate is 0.2 percent against 28.8 percent on coded rows, and they record zero injuries. That is the signature of a separate, minimally-coded reporting path, not of a set of harmless crashes; it is why severity is printed on the fully-coded basis alongside the all-reported basis (Finding 1) and why the unknown bucket is excluded from the collision comparisons (Finding 4).

Second, the vehicle-count field has a recording outage: a served value of 0 is read as not recorded everywhere (25,107 rows), and the 0 share is about 99 percent or more in December 2021-February 2022 and May-November 2022 against 0.0-0.5 percent in normal months. No figure in this brief uses the vehicle-count field across that window; the field's summary in stats.json is computed on rows with a value of 1 or more only. Third, the Property Damage field cannot support a split of the population: it is Y on 18,676 rows, N on exactly 1 row, and blank on the rest, so no figure uses it. One further row-level note: exactly one row (ObjectId 6) is an empty stub with only an accident number, a date, and an ObjectId served; it is counted in the 222,903 rows and excluded from every severity denominator.

## What this might mean

One reading of Findings 2 and 3 together is that two separate levers are visible in the same layer. Pedestrians are 30.2 percent of the people killed (280 of 926) while being involved in 1.5 percent of reported crashes, and the fatal-crash share per reported crash rises with darkness as coded, from 0.22 percent in daylight to 1.14 percent where the layer records dark-not-lighted conditions. On that reading, the pedestrian-signal and crossing measures and the lighting condition of corridors are the places where this dataset would show change first, and the January-October frame and the illumination table in this package are the baseline to measure against on the next pull.

The strongest alternative explanation applies to Finding 1 and tempers the rest: the mix of what gets reported may have changed. Reported volume dropped about 27 percent from 2019 to 2020 and never returned to the 2019 level, the March-June 2020 dip is visible in the monthly series, and minor-crash reporting thresholds and staffing are not in this dataset, so a smaller, more severe reported pool after 2019 would produce exactly the pattern in Finding 1 without any change in underlying driving. For Finding 3, the alternative is composition: dark-not-lighted crashes are concentrated on roads and at hours that differ from daylight crashes in speed environment and road type, and this layer does not measure either, so the 5-to-1 ratio is a severity gradient across reported-crash populations, not an estimate of what lighting alone changes. For Finding 2, the alternative is narrower: pedestrian involvement is read from harmful-event codes, and while the pedestrian code was verified against the description field exactly (3,330 both ways), a crash in which a pedestrian was present but not assigned the harmful-event code would not be counted here.

- **Nashville Department of Transportation and Multimodal Infrastructure (NDOT)**, which owns street lighting, signal timing, and pedestrian infrastructure, controls the levers Findings 2 and 3 point at. Concrete actions: report corridor lighting condition alongside the illumination table in this package when lighting work is programmed, so the dark-not-lighted fatal-crash share (1.14 percent of reported crashes, 143 fatal crashes) is the baseline the work is measured against; and evaluate the pedestrian signal changes now being installed (see the timing note below) against pedestrian-involved crashes at those intersections on the next pulls of this layer. Cost class: budget decision (lighting and signal programs are capital and operating budget items; no cost figure is asserted here). Outcome measure: the severity_by_illumination and vulnerable_road_users tables recomputed from this same layer on its daily refresh, using this package's code.
- **MNPD Traffic Division**, which owns crash-data coding in the TITAN system this layer is exported from, controls the field-level limits in Finding 8. Concrete actions: reduce the minimally-coded share (13,127 of 222,903 rows, 5.9 percent) and the exactly-00:00 time stamps (17,709 rows, 7.9 percent), and record what reporting path produces the minimally-coded rows. Cost class: low-cost operational change (coding practice and export configuration). Outcome measure: the minimally-coded count, the midnight-placeholder count, and the precinct not-recorded share on the next pull, all printed by this package's audit script.

## Data notes

- Snapshot and vintage: all figures are from the November 2, 2026 pull (222,903 records, server count equal to local count, immutable snapshot in the project warehouse). The same layer held 222,842 records at the November 1, 2026 study pull this brief refreshes, a drift of 61 records in one day; figures describe the layer as pulled on November 2, not any earlier or later state. Records are as reported at export; the metadata warns they are subject to change during investigation, so recent months are the least settled.
- Row bases: yearly counts in Finding 1 and in the year chart are on the known-count basis and sum to 222,902; the one stub row sits in 2020, which has 27,409 rows as served and 27,408 with counts populated. The layer total everywhere else is 222,903.
- Dates and times: Date_and_Time is a true UTC instant in this layer (all 24 UTC hours are present); every local figure reads it in America/Chicago. The metadata describes coverage as 2010-present, but the served layer starts January 1, 2019 (recorded as a coverage note in stats.json).
- Category stability: full Counters were recorded before any figure was computed (collision 11 values plus null; weather 14 plus null; illumination 8 plus null; precinct 9 served labels plus null, each summing to 222,903; audit output). No category rename or definition change inside the window is documented in the metadata; the breaks that do exist in the served view are completeness breaks, recorded where they bind (precinct in Finding 6, vehicle counts and the minimally-coded path in Finding 8, and a one-year weather gap: weather is null on 6,351 rows in 2020 against roughly 1,250-3,905 in other years, so weather splits crossing 2020 carry that gap, and no weather headline is made).
- Unit discipline: rows are crashes throughout. People injured and people killed are labeled as people wherever they appear and are never a numerator over crashes. Every rate's numerator and denominator are the same unit (crashes over crashes), asserted in the audit.
- Joins: no dataset join is used in this brief. Geography is the layer's own precinct and ZIP fields, and intersections are the layer's own address strings under the normalization recorded in Finding 6, with the Briley Pkwy name-order pair as the built-in control (439 plus 349 raw rows merge to 788 under the normalized key, asserted in the builder and the audit).
- Small numbers: percentages are printed with their underlying counts throughout. Groups under about 30 cases are flagged where they appear (DAWN fatal crashes 12 of 2,538; DUSK 15 of 3,946; the SMOG, SMOKE weather cell of 23 crashes is in the data panels with its small-n flag and is used in no finding).
- Official definitions: collision, weather, illumination, and harmful-event code meanings are the metadata's own appendices (Traffic Accidents Metadata v2.0, May 6, 2024, archived in this package as data/source-metadata.pdf). Vision Zero is NDOT's stated program framing on its own project pages; no figure derives from those pages.
- Named records: this brief names no individual person, address, or crash. Intersection and ZIP figures are aggregates over hundreds to thousands of records. Reporting_Officer values and raw street addresses stay in the warehouse layer only.
- Reproduction: code/pull_data.py records the pull (data/pull_log.json); code/audit_section10.py records the pre-flight audit (data/audit_output.txt); code/build_stats.py builds stats.json and the cleaned warehouse layer; code/verify_independent.py is the independent recomputation (data/verify_independent_output.txt, 51 of 51 checks passed); code/charts.py draws the charts; code/hero_composite.py composites the hero from stats.json and hero_source/hero-art.webp.
